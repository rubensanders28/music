Chords in a Key

A key is a family of 7 notes (a scale). Build a chord on each note using only notes from that family, and you get 7 chords that sound good together. Most songs use just a few of them.

Common progressions

A progression is the order chords are played in. These are used in countless songs.

Reading the Roman numerals: they number the chords 1 to 7 by which note of the scale they start on. Uppercase (IV) means a major chord, lowercase (iv) minor, ° diminished and + augmented. A ♭ in front (♭VII) means that chord sits a semitone lower than in the major scale. Because numerals don't depend on the key, "I – V – vi – IV" is the same progression in every key: that's how musicians talk about songs without naming a key.
