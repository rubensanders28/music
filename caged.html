The CAGED System

How the five open chords you already know, C, A, G, E and D, cover the whole guitar neck. Once you see it, you can play any chord in five places, and you always know where the root note is.

The idea in one minute

Every major chord can be played with the shape of one of the five open chords: C, A, G, E or D. Take the open shape, slide it up the neck, and let your first finger (or a barre) do the job of the nut. You already know this from barre chords: an F barre chord is the E shape moved up one fret, and a B♭ barre is the A shape moved up one fret.

The surprise is that the five shapes fit together like puzzle pieces, always in the same order: C → A → G → E → D → and back to C, an octave (12 frets) higher. Where one shape ends, the next one begins, and they share some notes. Together they cover the whole fretboard for that chord.

See it on the neck

The five shapes

In practice you rarely play all six strings of every shape: the C, G and D shapes are awkward as full barre chords. Guitarists use parts of them, like 3 or 4 strings near the root. The point of CAGED is to see where the chord notes are, not to fret every note.

Why it's useful

  • Any chord, five places. Stuck at the 7th fret? There's a shape of the chord you need right there.
  • Always know the root. Each shape has its root on known strings, so you can find any chord from its root note.
  • Solos that follow the chords. The chord notes in each shape are the safest notes to land on. Scales fit around the shapes too: the minor pentatonic positions on the Scale shapes page line up with them.
  • Play a song in one area of the neck, instead of jumping between open chords and barre chords.
  • Different sounds. The same chord sounds brighter higher up and fuller lower down, which is great for a second guitar part.

How to practise it

  1. Start with one chord. Choose C above and press each shape in turn: C, A, G, E, D. Play each one and say its name.
  2. Find the roots first. For every shape, find the root notes (the dots with a ring) before you play the chord.
  3. Walk up the neck. Play the chord in all five shapes, from low to high and back, without stopping.
  4. Change chords without moving. Pick a spot, say around the 5th fret, and play G, C and D there, using whichever shape each chord has nearby. Then try another spot.
  5. Small pieces. Play just the top 3 or 4 strings of each shape. That's how most players use it.
  6. Minor too. Switch to Minor: the same idea works with Cm, Am, Gm, Em and Dm shapes.
